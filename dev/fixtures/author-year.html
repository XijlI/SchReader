Synthetic symbiont survey shows host-driven turnover in aphid endosymbionts

Philippa A. Abbot, Patrick H. Degnan

Department of Synthetic Ecology, Example University, Exampletown, EX1 2AB, UK.

First published: 19 September 2005

Abstract

Endosymbiont communities of aphids were surveyed across 41 host species. Turnover between host clades was higher than turnover within clades, consistent with host-driven selection rather than vertical transmission alone.

Introduction

Early surveys of aphid endosymbionts established that a single obligate partner dominates each host species (Abbot & Moran 2002). Subsequent work extended this to secondary symbionts, which are patchily distributed (Moran et al. 2003). Two competing explanations remain: strict vertical transmission, or frequent horizontal acquisition followed by host filtering (Abbot 2002; Degnan & Moran 2004).

A third possibility is that symbiont lineages turn over on the same time scale as host diversification, which would make the two signals indistinguishable in cross-sectional data.

Materials and methods

Aphids were collected from 41 host plant species over three seasons. Diagnostic PCR was used to type each colony, following the protocol of Abbot and Moran (2002), with the modification described by Moran et al. (2003).

Statistical analysis

Turnover was partitioned with a beta-diversity decomposition (Degnan 2005).

Results

Of 612 colonies typed, 588 carried the expected obligate symbiont. Secondary symbiont carriage varied from 4% to 71% between host clades (Fig. 1). Turnover between clades exceeded within-clade turnover in every season, a pattern also visible in the pooled data (Fig. 2).

The relationship between clade age and turnover was weak, which is difficult to reconcile with a purely co-speciating model (Moran et al. 2003).

Discussion

Our results favour host filtering over strict co-speciation (Abbot & Moran 2002; Degnan & Moran 2004). The weak age signal is consistent with rapid turnover of facultative partners.
