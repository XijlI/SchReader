**English** · [中文](#chinese)

SchReader turns any publisher's article page into a clean, Nature-like reading layout — one text
column with a fixed **Sections / Figures / References** rail — as a Chrome (MV3) extension and as an
Android app. Both hosts share one extractor, one reader page and one library store.

## What is new in 0.4.2

### Springer Nature: the figure legend, and a sharp image

A Springer Nature figure is split across two elements: `<figcaption>` carries only the one-line
title ("Fig. 1: …") and the panel legend ("a, Schematic representation …") sits in a sibling
`div[data-test="bottom-caption"]`. The body walker emits the figure container whole, so everything the
caption element did not swallow disappeared — every Nature figure in the reading view had a bold title
and no legend at all.

- The extractor now merges every caption-ish block inside the figure container (innermost only, no
  image or table, nothing the primary caption already covers), and peels the "Fig. N:" label off the
  primary caption alone so a legend cannot lose its first words.
- Fig. 1 of a live paper: 124 → 1708 caption characters; the other four 717–2251.
- Antialiased images: the body markup only ever carries the 685 px copy (462 KB). Figures now load
  `/lw1200/` (1200 px, ~1.2 MB), the rail keeps the publisher's thumbnail, and **Expand** pulls the
  original `/full/` (2168 × 2060) only when you open it. Nothing is fetched, proxied or re-hosted —
  the size bucket in the URL is rewritten.
- Eight other publisher samples are unchanged section-for-section, figures, references and text
  length included.

### A phone keeps its own controls

- **The reference card stays put.** A tap fires `pointerover` *and* `pointerout` as the finger lifts,
  so a hover-driven card closed the moment it opened — it flashed and vanished while working per\-fectly
  on desktop. On touch, the card is now shown by the tap and dismissed by tapping anywhere else; the
  desktop hover preview is untouched.
- **The highlight pen and 「译」 are reachable.** Android's own selection menu (Copy / Share / Select
  all) floats directly above the selected text, exactly where those two buttons used to be seated.
  On touch they dock at the bottom of the viewport instead — centred, 78 px up so the app's bottom bar
  clears them, with room for the second button beside the first.

### The wordmark goes home

The `R SchReader` mark in the reading view's top bar is a button now: same destination as `▤` — My
library in the extension, the native shelf in the app. Its finger target is enlarged on a phone, where
the wordmark itself is hidden to leave the document title room.

### Under the hood

- `U.imgTier` (`util.js`) rewrites a Springer Nature size bucket; the figure model gains `big`, read
  only by the lightbox. `thumb` keeps the publisher's own URL, so the rail costs what it used to.
- `captionExtras` (`figures.js`) collects the sibling caption blocks; `reader.js`/`translate.js` share
  one `TOUCH` decision, so the two docked buttons cannot drift apart.
- `AGENTS.md` records all three as things not to break again.
