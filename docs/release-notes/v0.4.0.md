**English** · [中文](#chinese)

SchReader turns any publisher's article page into a clean, Nature-like reading layout — one text
column with a fixed **Sections / Figures / References** rail — as a Chrome (MV3) extension and as an
Android app. Both hosts share one extractor, one reader page and one library store.

## What is new in 0.4.0

### Saved words get a wordbook of their own

Select text while reading, press **译**, then the **★** on the translation card. The word, its
translation and the sentence it came from are filed into a **Words** list in your library
(**Saved / History / Words**).

- It is a vocabulary list, not a note on the article. Nothing is appended to the paper's note, and
  saving a word no longer adds the paper to your library — pressing ★ used to star a whole article
  just to give one word somewhere to live.
- Every row keeps the sentence the word appeared in, the paper it came from (clickable) and the date.
  Long example sentences are cut at a word boundary, never mid-word.
- Search across word, translation, example and source; **Copy** puts `word⇥translation` on the
  clipboard (two columns when pasted into a spreadsheet or an Anki import); Delete removes the row.
- The same word met twice in the same paper refreshes its entry instead of duplicating it; the same
  word from a different paper keeps its own example sentence.
- The list syncs through the same private gist as the library: a word saved on the phone shows up in
  Chrome and back, and a delete propagates too. On a device that has never synced it simply stays
  local.

### References: ranges, back-references and tables

- A printed range (`20–23`) rendered by the publisher as two links gave chips only to the endpoints,
  so 21 and 22 read "not cited in text" and could not be located at all — 4 of 43 references on one
  PNAS paper. Every number in the range is a chip of its own now; a number with no entry in the list
  stays plain text, and figure ranges ("Figs. 5–7") are left untouched.
- The `↑ cited 3×` count is its own row again: clicking it jumps to the in-text citation, and clicking
  again walks that reference's occurrences ("Citation 2 of 3"). It used to flash the reference entry
  and never move, and long jumps are instant instead of sweeping the page past your eyes.
- On Frontiers-shaped (JATS) pages the figure rail no longer invents entries: a label regex read the
  "V" of "Figure 2View in article" as a panel letter, so a four-figure paper listed eight — the same
  bug turned Wiley's "Figure 1Open in figure viewer" into "Figure 1O".
- `<sup>` exponents are no longer linked as citations ("1.41 × 10⁶ cells/mL" used to link reference
  6), citations inside table cells are linked, so references cited only in a table no longer read
  "not cited in text", and tables render as tables again instead of one run-on paragraph.
- Footer pruning now reaches behind link-only promo shells, and "the authors declare …" no longer
  counts as footer boilerplate — it opens the real conflict-of-interest and funding statements.

### Under the hood

- A new store key, `sr-words`, in `extension/library-store.js` (`words` / `addWord` / `patchWord` /
  `removeWord`) with the matching branch in the Android native store (`Library.kt`) and a `words`
  section in the gist merge. The three-way merge keeps deletes working, and the entry id is derived
  from the word plus the paper, so both devices compute the same id and a re-save updates in place.
- `dev/sync-sim.mjs` drives the wordbook through two isolated devices: save, sync, refresh in place,
  delete and a no-op sync that writes nothing.
- No new page, no new permission: the wordbook lives in the pages already mirrored into the APK by
  `android/tools/mirror.py`.
